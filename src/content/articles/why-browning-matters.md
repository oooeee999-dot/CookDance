Browning can bring deeper flavor, richer aroma, and a crisp surface. You do not need a laboratory to understand the everyday decisions that help it happen.

## Surface moisture matters

Water at the surface has to evaporate before the surface can get much hotter. Patting some ingredients dry and leaving space in the pan can help browning develop.

## Let food make contact

Constant stirring can interrupt contact with the pan. When browning is your aim, give food a little time before moving it.

## Observe, then adjust

Different ingredients brown at different rates. Use color and aroma as guides and lower the heat if darkening happens faster than the inside can cook. This article explains general principles; it does not present original CookDance test results.
